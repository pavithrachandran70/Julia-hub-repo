`exponent` on a rational now uses integer arithmetic, so values just below a power of two stay on the correct side of that boundary.

`Float64((2^60 - 1) // 2^60)` rounds to `1.0`, so the old `Real` fallback returned `0`. `exponent(::Rational)` in `base/math.jl` instead takes `floor(log2(|p/q|))` from `exponent` of the numerator and denominator, then subtracts one when `2^e` is still larger than `|p/q|`. That comparison is done with shifts of the absolute values, so `typemin` does not overflow. `1//1` is `0`, `2//1` is `1`, and `(2^60 - 1) // 2^60` is `-1`. Zero and infinite rationals still throw `DomainError`. Float and integer methods are unchanged.

The formula in the story, subtracting 1 whenever `|p| < |q|`, is one too low on that boundary (`59 - 60 - 1 = -2`) and on `3//5`. The shift test matches `2^e <= |p/q| < 2^(e+1)`.
